Welcome to the DFM FAQ. If you have questions about our mission, hardware builds, or general opsec, you're in the right place.

## General

<details>
<summary>What is Don't Feed Machines?</summary>

DFM is a repository of counter-surveillance and privacy hardware builds. We provide open-source tools and documentation to help you reclaim autonomy from pervasive tracking and data collection.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Is this legal?</summary>

All information provided in the DFM wiki is for educational purposes. The legality of deploying specific counter-surveillance hardware (like RF jammers or laser microphone defeaters) varies heavily by jurisdiction. You are strictly responsible for knowing and adhering to your local laws.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Why is the content open-source?</summary>

We believe that privacy and autonomy are fundamental rights, not luxury products. By open-sourcing our hardware and research under the CC BY-SA 4.0 license, we ensure these tools remain accessible, auditable, and free from corporate control.
</details>

## Technical

<details>
<summary>Do I need a background in electronics to build these projects?</summary>

Not necessarily. While some advanced projects (like custom PCBs) require soldering and basic electronic knowledge, many others are plug-and-play, utilize off-the-shelf components, or require only basic assembly. Each project in the toolkit is rated by difficulty.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Where do I source the components?</summary>

Each hardware project includes a Bill of Materials (BOM) with generic component specifications. We prioritize parts that are widely available globally from standard electronics distributors to avoid supply-chain bottlenecks.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Can I contribute my own hardware designs?</summary>

Absolutely. DFM is built on community contributions. You can submit pull requests to our GitHub repositories or share your designs in our community channels once they are established.
</details>
