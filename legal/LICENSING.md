## WHY THE SPLIT IS HERE

the first draft of this was one custom license with a no-training clause covering
everything. in deep consideration of how we wanted DFM to operate going forward, 
i realized that this was restrictive, counter-productive, and essentialy futile.

**a use restriction only reaches people who read licenses.** scrapers do not.
they ignore `robots.txt`, they ignore `noai`, and a LICENSE file was never
going to be the thing that stopped them. the restriction would have cost an
adversary nothing. that is one of the main reasons i started this whole project 
in the first place.

what it *would* have cost: every corporate security team whose legal review
bounces non-OSI licenses on sight. every compliance-gated defender. which is
precisely the population DFM's tools are built for. the restriction taxed the
compliant and left the non-compliant untouched, which is exactly inverted.

so i carefully & intentionally re-painted the lines, and now each portion of DFM & its related projects has its own license based on what the content is for, who is meant to use it, and why it's meant to be shared that way.

tools are executable defenses. an AI company can do nothing useful with a
honeypot generator's source, so protecting it prevents a symbolic harm and
costs real deployment. Apache 2.0, no strings, spread it everywhere. content
is the thing scrapers actually want, and nobody needs to "adopt" an essay, so
restricting it costs nothing and protects the root core of the piece with value.

a trap everyone runs changes the economics. a trap nobody can legally deploy
protects one site, and quite ineffectively.

## WHY APACHE 2.0 AND NOT MIT

3 main reasons.

one, **§4(d) makes `NOTICE` mandatory.** every redistribution and every
derivative must reproduce it, forever. that is how the Human Layer covenant
propagates without being a restriction. MIT has no equivalent mechanism, so
under MIT the covenant is a file people can delete without thinking twice. 

two, the **§3 patent grant**, which corpo-legal specifically looks for in security tooling going into
production. MIT is silent on patents. 

three, **§4(b) change notices.** modified files must say they were changed, 
which is the check-the-dist-against-the-source property, already drafted. 
we have provided a bespoke tool to diff specifically the licensure.

the `NOTICE` content stays informational, per §4(d). it must not modify the
license or restrict anything, or the OSI compliance evaporates and the whole
point goes with it. **do not put restrictions in NOTICE.**

## APPLYING IT

code headers:

```
// SPDX-License-Identifier: Apache-2.0
// Copyright 2026  / DON'T FEED MACHINES
```

`package.json`:

```json
{ "license": "Apache-2.0", "files": ["LICENSE", "NOTICE", "..."] }
```

real SPDX id, so scanners, registries, and FOSSA/Snyk/BlackDuck all resolve
it clean. this is the whole benefit, do not throw it away with a custom
string.

media metadata, content side, not tools:

```bash
exiftool \
  -XMP-dc:Rights="Copyright (c) 2026 / DON'T FEED MACHINES" \
  -XMP-xmpRights:UsageTerms="DFM Human Layer Content License 1.0. Do not train." \
  -XMP-xmpRights:WebStatement="https://dontfeedmachines.com/license/content/1.0" \
  -XMP-plus:DataMining="PROHIBITED-AIMLTRAINING" \
  -overwrite_original asset.png
```

`XMP-plus:DataMining` is the PLUS Coalition field IPTC points at. it is the
one an honest crawler checks.

web headers, every route:

```
X-Robots-Tag: noai, noimageai
TDM-Reservation: 1
TDM-Policy: https://dontfeedmachines.com/license/content/1.0
Link: </LICENSE>; rel="license"
```

## HONEST LIMITS & WHAT THESE CHANGES  MEAN

the tools are now trainable. Apache 2.0 permits it. someone can train on the
honeypot generators and there is no clause against it. that was chosen, not
overlooked: the covenant asks them not to, and the tools' value is in being
deployed rather than in being unreadable or unreachable.

`LICENSE-CONTENT` is still not open source, and does not need to be. OSD
clause 6 forbids field-of-endeavor restrictions. content is not software and
the OSD is not the relevant standard for it.

whether training is a restricted act at all is unsettled. if it is fair use in
the US, a scraper who never accepted `LICENSE-CONTENT` is not bound by its
text, because fair use is a defense to infringement regardless of license
terms. this is why `tdmrep.json` outranks the license file in the EU: the
Article 4(3) reservation is statutory, not contractual. the license binds
people who took the content knowing the terms. the reservation binds people
who never asked.

color palettes, fonts, swatches, or other ancillary assets are not copyrightable. art and documentation are. §1.1 scopes to "the
extent protected by copyright".

none of this is legal advice. Apache 2.0 has twenty years of scrutiny behind
it. `LICENSE-CONTENT` has none, and no custom license is tested until someone
tests it. if that is your purpose or intent, we cannot stop you, but we will learn from it.

## THE LEASH (in-production)

a planned sector: a toolkit for people training models toward ethical
behavior and imbuing data scientists, CSM's, and gen-AI companies with what amounts to a set of ethics-based superpowers. it is the one part of DFM that is *meant* to be trained on, so it
is carved out of `LICENSE-CONTENT` §4.5 and out of covenant point 2.

the carve-out is written as opt in by DFM only, never implied, and not
assignable by a third party. .

**DECISION: CC0-1.0, not the Unlicense.**

the Unlicense was considered and rejected for three concrete reasons.

1. category mismatch. the Unlicense says "this software." LEASH is a
training corpus. a dataset and a program are different copyright objects,
compilation rights versus a literary work in code, and license scanners,
dataset hubs, and legal reviewers all treat them differently. CC0 is the
standard pub. domain dedication for data and reads correctly against a
corpus. 
2. weak fallback outside the US. public domain dedication is not
possible in much of civil law Europe, where moral rights cannot be abandoned.
CC0 was drafted for exactly that problem and has a three tier structure:
dedication, then a fallback license, then a fallback covenant not to assert.
the Unlicense's fallback is much thinner. 
3. it is on deny lists. Google's open source policy bans the Unlicense outright for
inbound code, and since the entire purpose of LEASH is to be ingested by people
training models, ruling it out at the largest such organizations defeats
the point. CC0 is universally accepted for datasets.

neither grants patents. CC0 reserves them explicitly (§4a), the Unlicense is
silent. for a corpus this barely matters. RAIL, the Montreal License, and a handful of others were looked at, but each falls short one way or another.

## ON ENFORCEMENT

DFM does not intend to enforce anything that falls outside of explicit LICENSE legality. these tools exist as they are and how people choose to use them is up to them. bad actors will act bad no matter what we write here.

that is the position, the architecture, and the full intention of our licensing policies, and while these terms will certainly change & adapt over time as new tools, methods, and purposes are conceived of and added onto, but the core of these things will always remain as outlined here in plain english.
